# The Network+ N10-009 exam explained

CompTIA Network+ is a vendor-neutral certification for the networking skills every IT role leans on: how networks are built, run, secured and fixed. These notes cover the **N10-009** exam (Network+ V9), which launched on June 20, 2024. CompTIA says exams usually retire about three years after launch and estimates 2027 for this one, so N10-009 is the version to study now.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Exam code | N10-009 (Network+ V9) |
| Launched | June 20, 2024 |
| Retires | Usually three years after launch (CompTIA's estimate: 2027) |
| Questions | A maximum of 90 |
| Question types | Multiple-choice and performance-based |
| Time | 90 minutes |
| Passing score | 720 on a scale of 100 to 900 |
| Recommended experience | CompTIA A+ plus 9 to 12 months of hands-on experience as a junior network administrator or network support technician |
| Languages | English, German, Japanese, Portuguese, Spanish |
| Delivery | Pearson VUE test center, or online with OnVUE |
| Renewal | Three-year cycle; 30 continuing education units (CEUs) |

Sources: [CompTIA Network+ page](https://www.comptia.org/en-us/certifications/network/), the [official N10-009 objectives](https://lecbyo.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/35a7403ab73211ef9dcda6f347fbf652), and the CompTIA policy pages listed in [Sources](sources.md), checked 24 November 2026.

## The five domains and their weights

| Domain | Weight | Roughly how many questions (our estimate) | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 Networking Concepts | 23% | about 20 of up to 90 | [Domain 1](domains/1-networking-concepts.md) |
| 2.0 Network Implementation | 20% | about 18 | [Domain 2](domains/2-network-implementation.md) |
| 3.0 Network Operations | 19% | about 17 | [Domain 3](domains/3-network-operations.md) |
| 4.0 Network Security | 14% | about 12 | [Domain 4](domains/4-network-security.md) |
| 5.0 Network Troubleshooting | 24% | about 21 | [Domain 5](domains/5-network-troubleshooting.md) |

The weights are CompTIA's. The question counts are our arithmetic (weight times 90, rounded down), because CompTIA publishes only the maximum number of questions, not how many come from each domain.

Two things follow from the weights. Network Troubleshooting is the single heaviest domain at 24%, and it draws on everything else: you cannot troubleshoot a DHCP failure or a VLAN mismatch you do not understand. And Networking Concepts, at 23%, holds the OSI model, ports, addressing and subnetting that every other domain assumes, so it is the right place to start. Together those two are nearly half the exam.

## Who the exam is for

Network+ suits people who work with networks day to day or want to: help desk and desktop support staff moving toward network roles, junior network administrators, and system administrators who need a firm grip on the network underneath their servers. CompTIA names technical support, network operations and system administration as the job roles it fits, and lists technical support specialist, network operations specialist and system administrator among the NICE and DoD 8140 work roles.

Many people take Network+ after A+ and before Security+, since Security+ assumes the networking covered here.

## Recommended experience

CompTIA recommends A+ plus 9 to 12 months of hands-on experience in a junior network administrator or network support technician role. That is a recommendation, not an entry requirement. No other exam has to be passed first, and you can book N10-009 without A+. If you skip A+, expect to spend extra time on the basics A+ covers, such as cabling, IP configuration on a client and the command line.

## How the questions work

- **Multiple-choice.** Most questions. Some have one correct answer; others ask you to choose two or more. Read the stem for the number.
- **Performance-based questions (PBQs).** Short hands-on tasks in a simulated environment or a drag-and-drop layout: for example, filling in a subnetting table, matching ports to protocols, configuring a switch port or putting troubleshooting steps in order. They usually take longer than multiple-choice questions. See [Performance-based questions and pacing](performance-based-questions.md).
- **Scenario wording.** Many stems include words like BEST, MOST likely, FIRST or NEXT. More than one option may be partly right; your job is to pick the one that best fits the stated goal or constraint. Troubleshooting questions in particular often ask what to do NEXT, which depends on where you are in the method.

## How the objectives document works

CompTIA's objectives document lists every objective (for example "5.1 Explain the troubleshooting methodology") with bulleted examples under each. CompTIA notes that these bulleted lists are **not exhaustive**: other examples of technologies, processes or tasks for an objective may appear on the exam, even if the document does not list them. Treat the bullets as the minimum, and understand each idea well enough to apply it to a network you have not seen.

Watch the verbs. "Compare and contrast" means you must tell similar things apart, such as single-mode and multimode fiber or SFP and QSFP transceivers. "Explain" and "Summarize" mean you must know what something is and why it matters. "Given a scenario" means you will apply it to a situation: 10 of the 25 objectives start that way, including four of the five in Domain 5.

The document also includes an acronym list. CompTIA encourages candidates to get a working knowledge of every acronym on it. Our [glossary](glossary.md) covers the ones that matter most.

## Booking and taking the exam

You can sit the exam at a Pearson VUE test center or online through OnVUE. Online exams are recorded through your webcam, have no scheduled breaks, and can be rescheduled or cancelled only until the appointment's start time. The full checklist is on the [exam-day page](exam-day-checklist.md).

## Retakes and renewal

- **Retakes.** No waiting period between your first and second attempt. Before a third or later attempt you must wait at least 14 calendar days from your last attempt. Each attempt is paid in full, and there are no free retests.
- **Renewal.** Network+ is renewed on a three-year cycle through CompTIA's continuing education program, which requires 30 CEUs for Network+.

## Practice the real format

Reading teaches the content; timed practice teaches the pace.
