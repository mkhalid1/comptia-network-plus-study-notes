# FAQ

Real questions people search for about Network+, answered briefly. Facts come from CompTIA's own pages, listed in [Sources](sources.md). Where an answer is our opinion or study advice, it says so.

## Is Network+ hard to pass?

CompTIA publishes no pass rates, so nobody can give you an official difficulty figure. In our view, the hard parts are subnetting under time pressure, the sheer number of ports, protocols and acronyms, and troubleshooting scenarios that ask what to do NEXT. People who already configure IP addresses, switches or Wi-Fi at work usually find it much easier than those starting cold. The [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) will show you quickly where you stand.

## What's harder, Network+ or A+?

There is no official answer, since CompTIA publishes no pass rates for either. As our view: A+ is broader (two exams covering hardware, operating systems, security and support), while Network+ is one exam that goes deeper into a single subject. Many people find Network+ harder because of subnetting and protocol detail, especially if they have never worked on a network. If you have done A+, you already know the client side of networking, which helps a lot.

## What is the hardest CompTIA certification to get?

CompTIA does not rank its certifications by difficulty and publishes no pass rates, so any ranking you read is opinion. What we can say is that CompTIA's recommended experience grows along the usual path: 9 to 12 months of networking work plus A+ for Network+, and Network+ plus two years in a security or systems administrator role for Security+. "Hardest" also depends on you: an experienced security analyst may find a security exam easier than a networking one. Pick the certification that matches the job you want, not the one with the toughest reputation.

## Can I skip A+ and do Network+?

Yes. CompTIA recommends A+ plus 9 to 12 months of hands-on networking experience, but that is a recommendation, not a requirement. You can book N10-009 without having passed A+. If you skip it, plan extra time for the basics A+ covers, such as cabling, client IP settings and command-line tools. See [who the exam is for](exam-overview.md#who-the-exam-is-for).

## What's the difference between N10-008 and N10-009?

N10-008 is the earlier version of Network+. N10-009 launched on June 20, 2024, with its own objectives and five domains, and it is the version these notes cover. If you are studying from older material, check that it follows the N10-009 objectives, because the exam you sit tests those. See the [exam overview](exam-overview.md#the-five-domains-and-their-weights).

## How many questions are on Network+, and how long is it?

A maximum of 90 questions, a mix of multiple-choice and performance-based, in 90 minutes. See the [exam overview](exam-overview.md).

## What is the passing score?

720 on a scale of 100 to 900. It is a scaled score, so it does not convert to a fixed percentage of questions. See [scoring](scoring.md).

## How much does the Network+ cert cost?

CompTIA sets the price, and it varies by country and changes from time to time, so check the current price on CompTIA's Network+ page or store before you book. Each attempt is paid in full; there are no free retests.

## How long should I study for Network+?

It depends on your starting point. As our guidance: someone who already works in IT support and handles basic networking can often be ready in four to six weeks of steady study. Someone new to IT should plan for longer, often two to three months, with time for hands-on practice. Use the [study plans](study-plans.md) and stretch each day into a week if the material is new.

## Is CompTIA still worth it in 2026?

In our view, for entry-level and early-career IT roles, yes: CompTIA certifications are vendor-neutral and appear in many job postings for support, network and security roles. CompTIA also lists Network+ against NICE and DoD 8140 work roles such as technical support specialist, network operations specialist and system administrator. Whether it is worth it for you depends on the jobs you are targeting. Read a dozen postings for the role you want and see how often it appears.

## Is CompTIA Network+ enough to get a job?

On its own, a certification rarely lands a job, in our view. Network+ shows an employer you understand networking, and CompTIA names technical support, network operations and system administration as the roles it fits. Employers also look for hands-on skills: a home lab, help desk experience, and the ability to explain how you fixed something. Pay depends on the role and location.

## Should I take Network+ before Security+?

CompTIA recommends Network+ as part of the experience for Security+, so taking Network+ first is the common path. Security+ assumes you understand ports, protocols, VLANs and firewalls, all of which Network+ covers. Neither exam is a formal requirement for the other.

## Which CompTIA certs never expire?

Under CompTIA's continuing education program, A+, Network+ and Security+ all renew on a three-year cycle, so none of these three is a lifetime certification. Check CompTIA's continuing education pages for other certifications.

## How long is Network+ valid?

Network+ is renewed on a three-year cycle through CompTIA's continuing education program. You need 30 continuing education units (CEUs) in that cycle.

## Can I take Network+ online?

Yes. You can test at a Pearson VUE test center or online through OnVUE. Online exams are recorded through your webcam and have no scheduled breaks. See the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md).

## Are there performance-based questions?

Yes. The exam is a mix of multiple-choice and performance-based questions. CompTIA does not publish how many PBQs you will get or how they are scored. See [performance-based questions and pacing](performance-based-questions.md), which includes a subnetting table and a troubleshooting-order task to try.

## What happens if I fail?

You can retake it. There is no waiting period between your first and second attempt; before a third or later attempt, you must wait at least 14 calendar days. Your score report lists the objectives linked to questions you got wrong, which is the best place to start your next round of study.
