# Study plans: 7 days and 2 days

Two plans: one for the final week before your exam, and one for when the exam is two days away. Both start with the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) so you know where your marks are leaking.

A word of honesty first, as our guidance: seven days is enough to **finish** preparing, not to start from nothing. If most of the topics on the domain pages are new to you, stretch each day of the 7-day plan into a week of study, and keep the order. The plan is built around the weights: Network Troubleshooting (24%) and Networking Concepts (23%) get the most time.

## 7-day plan

Expect 1.5 to 3 hours a day.

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Read the [exam overview](exam-overview.md). Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) under time. Note your two weakest domains, weighted by how much each is worth. |
| 2 | Domain 1 (23%) | Read [Networking Concepts](domains/1-networking-concepts.md) in full. Do all 4 items. Write the OSI layers and the port table from memory, then do ten subnetting problems on paper. |
| 3 | Domain 5 (24%) | Read [Network Troubleshooting](domains/5-network-troubleshooting.md). Do all 4 items. Write the seven troubleshooting steps from memory, and for each command-line tool note one problem it helps you find. |
| 4 | Domains 2 and 3 (20% and 19%) | Read [Network Implementation](domains/2-network-implementation.md) and [Network Operations](domains/3-network-operations.md). Do both item sets. Make flash cards for routing protocols, wireless standards, DNS record types and RPO vs RTO. |
| 5 | Domain 4 (14%) and first timed run | Read [Network Security](domains/4-network-security.md) and do its items. Then take a full timed practice test, such as the [free Network+ (N10-009) practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/network-plus?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=network-plus). Score it by domain. |
| 6 | Fix the weakest heavy domain | Go back to the weakest domain from day 5. Reread its "Traps that cost points" and redo its items without the answers. Read [performance-based questions and pacing](performance-based-questions.md). |
| 7 | Second timed run and rest | Take a second timed test in the morning. Compare domain scores with day 5. Read the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md). Stop studying by early evening. |

### How to review a missed item

Most of your improvement comes from reviewing misses properly. For each one, answer three questions:

1. **What did I confuse?** For example: /27 vs /28, SFTP vs FTPS, RPO vs RTO, a switching loop vs a broadcast storm.
2. **Which word in the stem should have stopped me?** For example: "FIRST", "NEXT", "only one floor", "after the change".
3. **What will I check next time?** One short habit, such as "find the layer before choosing the tool" or "write the block size before the network ID".

Keep the answers in one running list. By day 6, the same few confusions will keep appearing, and fixing those is worth more than doing another hundred new questions.

## 2-day plan

For when the exam is already booked and close.

### Day 1: find and fix the big gaps

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 min | Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | 40 min | Read "Key concepts explained" in [Domain 5](domains/5-network-troubleshooting.md), the heaviest domain, focusing on the troubleshooting method and the command-line tools. |
| 3 | 30 min | Subnetting drill: twenty network ID and broadcast problems from /24 to /30, timed. |
| 4 | 45 min | Read "Traps that cost points" on all five domain pages, then do the practice items for your two weakest domains. Read every explanation. |
| 5 | 60 to 90 min | Take a full timed practice test, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/network-plus?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=network-plus). |
| 6 | 30 min | Review every miss with the three questions above. |

### Day 2: sit the exam

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 min | Redo yesterday's misses without the answers. |
| 2 | 10 min | Write the port table and the block-size table once from memory as a warm-up. |
| 3 | 10 min | Read [performance-based questions and pacing](performance-based-questions.md) and the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md). |
| 4 | Exam | Sit the exam rested and on time. |

## What not to do

- **Do not use brain dumps.** CompTIA's authorized materials use policy says people who use "brain dump" material (unauthorized copies of real exam content) will have their certifications revoked and be suspended from future testing. Beyond the risk, they teach answers to someone else's questions, not the reasoning you need for yours.
- **Do not study the wrong version.** Older Network+ material may follow different objectives. Check that a book, course or video is written for N10-009.
- **Do not skip subnetting because it feels slow.** It is one of the few skills that can be drilled until it is automatic, and it shows up as both multiple-choice and PBQ tasks.
- **Do not memorize port numbers without the protocols' purpose.** Many questions describe what a service does and expect you to name the port, or the reverse.
- **Do not cram late the night before.** Tired readers miss words like FIRST, NEXT and NOT, which is where many marks go.
